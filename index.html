Choose your route

Your study options

There is no single right way in. Pick the option that matches your results, your English score and your goals. The examples use UNSW and UNSW College, but most Australian universities offer similar choices.

After high school

OptionDirect entry

Start the bachelor's degree

Go straight into first year if your high school results and English score meet the university's entry requirements.

Suits you if
You have strong results and an English test score at the degree's level.
Length
The full degree, often 3 to 4 years (engineering honours is usually 4).

Scholarship note: the university must meet your track's ranking rule (top 30 for Al-Ruwwad, top 200 for Imdad).

OptionAbout 1 year

Foundation Studies

A preparatory year that bridges high school and university. You study subjects linked to your degree and learn how Australian study works, then enter first year.

Suits you if
Your high school certificate is not accepted for direct entry, or you want a softer start.
Leads to
First year of a bachelor's degree, if you meet the progression requirements.

Scholarship note: from 2025–26, the Ministry asks for at least 90% in high school and IELTS 5.5 (or equivalent) to be sponsored for a preparatory year.

OptionAbout 1 year

Diploma

Covers first-year university content in smaller classes with more support. If you meet the progression requirements, you enter the second year of a matching degree.

Suits you if
You already know your field, such as engineering or business, and want extra support in your first year.
Leads to
Second year of a linked degree, with credit transfer.

Scholarship note: get SACM approval for the diploma and the degree it leads to before you accept the offer.

OptionWeeks to months

English course

An academic English program that raises your score to the level your program needs. Some programs let you skip this with a high enough test score.

Suits you if
Your English score is below the entry level of your chosen program.
Can be combined with
Any option above, as a package offer.

Scholarship note: SACM does not sponsor English courses. You pay for this yourself, so try to reach the English score you need before you start.

After a bachelor's degree

Option1 to 2 years

Master's degree

Coursework or research master's for graduates. Entry depends on your bachelor's GPA, field and English score.

Suits you if
You meet the GPA and English requirements of the master's program.

Scholarship note: Al-Ruwwad (top 30) and Imdad (top 200) both include master's study. See Master's, pre-master's and PhD.

OptionShort program

Pre-master's

A preparation program for graduates who need to strengthen their academic skills or English before a master's degree.

Suits you if
You are close to the master's entry requirements but not there yet.

Scholarship note: pre-master's is not on the list of programs SACM sponsors, so expect to pay for it yourself unless SACM confirms otherwise.

Option3 to 4 years

PhD

A research degree. You write a thesis on your own research, guided by a supervisor.

Suits you if
You have a strong master's or honours degree and a research idea you want to follow.
Start by
Finding a supervisor and writing a short research proposal.

Scholarship note: PhDs are sponsored through the Research & Development track. See Master's, pre-master's and PhD.

Important: SACM sponsors Foundation, Diploma, Bachelor's, Master's and PhD programs. English courses are not sponsored, so you pay for them yourself.

Entry scores, program lengths and progression rules change each year and differ between programs. Always check the current rules on the official websites of UNSW College and your university before you apply.

After your bachelor's

Master's, pre-master's and PhD

SACM also sponsors postgraduate study. The rules are different from undergraduate study, so start planning early.

Master's degree الماجستير

  • Two typesCoursework master's (classes and exams, sometimes a project) or research master's (a thesis with a supervisor, often called a Master by Research or MPhil).
  • LengthA coursework master's usually takes 1 to 2 years full time. The Ministry's FAQ says the scholarship covers a master's according to the university's system, up to 2 years.
  • Scholarship tracksAl-Ruwwad (top 30 universities, any field) and Imdad (top 200, in fields the job market needs).
  • EntryUniversities look at your bachelor's GPA, your field and your English score. The Ministry needs an unconditional offer, so meet the English requirement before you apply for the scholarship.
  • Changing fieldThe Ministry's FAQ says your master's does not have to be in the same field as your bachelor's.

Pre-master's ما قبل الماجستير

  • What it isA short preparation program for graduates who are close to the master's entry requirements. Pathway colleges often offer it.
  • SponsorshipPre-master's is not on the list of programs SACM sponsors (Foundation, Diploma, Bachelor's, Master's and PhD). Expect to pay for it yourself unless SACM confirms otherwise in writing.
  • Before you enrolThe scholarship needs an unconditional master's offer, so check that finishing the pre-master's gives you one.

PhD الدكتوراه

  • Scholarship trackResearch & Development, which is for PhDs only. You need admission from the top 200 universities on the program's list for your field.
  • Find a supervisor firstAustralian PhDs are research degrees. Contact academics who work in your area, share a short research proposal, and ask whether they can supervise you before you apply.
  • LengthAustralian PhDs usually take 3 to 4 years full time. The Ministry's FAQ says the scholarship covers a PhD according to the university's system, up to 3 years, so ask SACM early how extensions work.
  • Offer and study modeYou need a final, unconditional offer, and you must study in person on campus.

Bringing your family المرافقون

  • Family membersThe Ministry's FAQ says family members can join you, and a companion who lives with you in Australia receives entitlements under the regulations.
  • MahramThe FAQ says a mahram is not required.
  • HousingFamilies usually need a whole apartment. See Finding a place for Domain and realestate.com.au.
  • EmployeesGovernment and private sector employees can apply if they meet the general and track conditions.

Scholarship guide

The SACM scholarship, explained

Most Saudi students in Australia study on the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques Scholarship Program, run by the Ministry of Education. You apply through the Ministry using the Qubool platform, and once you are in Australia the Saudi Arabian Cultural Mission (SACM) in Canberra manages your scholarship.

The four scholarship tracks مسارات الابتعاث

Al-Ruwwadمسار الرواد
Top 30 universities · Bachelor's, Master's

For high school and university graduates who gain admission to one of the world's leading universities, in any field.

Imdadمسار إمداد
Top 200 · Bachelor's, Master's

For fields the Saudi job market needs. The list of approved majors is set each year.

Research & Developmentمسار البحث والتطوير
Top 200 in the field · PhD only

For PhD study in national research priority areas.

Wa'idمسار واعد
Linked to a job · Diplomas to degrees

Training and study tied to employment with a Saudi company after you graduate. Australia is one of the listed countries.

Before you apply قبل التقديم

  • Who can applySaudi citizens of good conduct. There is no general age limit for most tracks.
  • Conditions on your offerThe Ministry asks for an unconditional offer for a bachelor's, master's or PhD, studied full time at the university's main campus. Executive programs are not accepted, and qualifications from outside Saudi Arabia need equivalency first.
  • Documents you needA valid national ID card (chip card), a passport valid for at least one year, your registered national address, certified graduation certificate and transcripts, equivalency for any foreign qualification, and your English and other test results.
  • Check the universityApproved universities and fields can change each year. Use the Ministry's recommended universities search, its Australia page and the 2026–2027 university lists by field.
  • AdmissionYou need an offer from a university that meets your track's rules, in a major on that year's approved list.
  • Study modeYou must study full time and live in the country of study.
  • Preparatory yearFrom 2025–26: at least 90% high school average and IELTS 5.5 (or equivalent).
  • What SACM sponsorsFoundation, Diploma, Bachelor's, Master's and PhD programs.
  • English coursesNot sponsored. English language courses are at your own cost, so aim to meet the English requirement before you start your program.
  • Aptitude testsThe Ministry says applications do not require aptitude or achievement test scores, although some Wa'id programs ask for them.
  • Where to applyNew applicants apply through the National Unified Admission Platform, Qubool (منصة قبول), signing in with Nafath. Safeer (منصة سفير) is where you manage your scholarship once you are a scholarship student. Selection is based on merit and available places.
  • Application datesIn the 2026 cycle, applications opened in early February and closed on 7 May 2026, and results were announced on 12–14 June 2026. The Ministry announces the dates for each new cycle, so follow Qubool and the Ministry's official accounts.
  • Already self-funded?You can apply to join the scholarship if you meet the general and track conditions. The Ministry also has a "join the scholarship" e-service with rules on university ranking, GPA and units already completed.

While you study in Australia أثناء الدراسة

  • What is usually coveredTuition, health cover (OSHC) and a monthly allowance (see the monthly allowance). Your exact entitlements are in your scholarship decision.
  • Financial guarantee letterYour university needs a letter from SACM confirming what it pays. Most universities ask you to upload it when you accept your offer, and then invoice SACM directly.
  • New letter for each changeMoving from Foundation to a diploma, or from a diploma to a degree, or needing extra time, usually means a new guarantee letter. Request it early so your enrolment is not delayed.
  • Changing program or majorGet SACM approval before you change. Only approved programs and majors are funded.
  • Results every termUpload your results to Safeer after each term and keep your academic progress up. Poor progress can stop your allowance or end the scholarship.
  • Dropped or failed coursesYou may have to pay for courses you withdraw from late or repeat. Ask SACM before you drop a course.
  • Keep your recordsSave every letter as a PDF: offer, CoE, enrolment status letter, credit transfer outcome and transcripts. SACM may ask for proof of your program and start date at each stage.
  • Contact SACMThe Saudi Arabian Cultural Mission is at 16 Watson Street, Turner ACT 2612 (Canberra), phone +61 2 6232 5978. Visit the SACM Australia website.

After you graduate بعد التخرج

  • Degree equivalencyTo use your degree in Saudi Arabia, apply for equivalency (معادلة الشهادة) with the Ministry of Education. Full-time study and living in Australia during your degree matter here, so keep your transcripts and travel records.
  • Professional bodiesSome careers have their own registration. For example, engineers register with the Saudi Council of Engineers.

A place to live

Finding a place in Sydney

My advice: do not sign a long rental from overseas. Book a short stay first, then find your real home once you can see it in person.

  • Before you flyBook about 2 weeks in an Airbnb (or similar short stay) near your campus. It gives you a safe base while you look.
  • When you arriveStart searching straight away. Message several places, book inspections, and visit rooms in person before you pay anything.
  • Before 2 weeks endChoose a place, confirm the move-in date in writing, and move before your short stay finishes. Extend the Airbnb a few days if you need more time.
Room in a shared homeFast and flexible

Flatmates

Rooms in shared houses and apartments. You usually deal directly with the people living there.

Lease and application
Usually no lease to sign and no formal rental application. You can often move in within days.
Best for
Your first place in Sydney, and students who want to share costs.
Watch out for
Still ask for a simple written agreement that states the rent, bond, bills and notice period.

Location

Live near transport

Check how long the trip to campus takes. Popular student areas near UNSW: Kensington, Kingsford, Randwick.

Safety

Avoid rental scams

Never pay a deposit for a room you have not seen, and never send money to someone who says they are overseas. If a deal looks too cheap, it usually is.

Bond

Protect your bond

For a lease through an agent, your bond should be lodged with NSW Fair Trading. Take dated photos of the room when you move in.

Free help

Get a second opinion

Not sure about a lease or a landlord? Arc's free legal service gives students advice on renting and other problems.

Practical matters

Money and work

Work limits

Know your visa rules

Student visa holders have a limit on work hours during study periods, currently 48 hours per fortnight. Rules can change, so check Home Affairs. Sponsored students should also check their sponsor's rules on part-time work.

Pay

Know your rights

International students have the same workplace rights as everyone else in Australia, including minimum wage. The Fair Work Ombudsman explains your rights for free.

Tax

Get a Tax File Number

If you start working, apply for a Tax File Number (TFN) online through the Australian Taxation Office. It is free.

Budget

Plan for rent first

Rent is usually the biggest cost in Sydney. Use the budget planner to see how much of your allowance it takes, and read Finding a place.

From experience

What I learned on the way

  • Keep every official letter

    When you move from Foundation to a diploma, or from a diploma to a degree, your sponsor may ask for proof of your new program, your start date, your credit transfer outcome and an enrolment letter. Save each one as a PDF the day you receive it.

  • Ask about your credit transfer early

    Your credit outcome decides which courses you take in your first term at university. Knowing it early helps you plan a full study load.

  • Your English improves fastest outside class

    Clubs, volunteering and part-time work give you daily practice. Do not wait until you feel confident to join.

  • Use the free support

    Universities and student organisations offer free help with study skills, wellbeing and legal questions such as renting. Use it before a small problem becomes a big one.
